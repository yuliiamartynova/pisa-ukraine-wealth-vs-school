Family or school? How the social make-up of schools shapes Ukrainian students' achievement

Students from better-off families score higher in PISA. But in Ukraine, the school a student attends, and above all who else studies there, matters more than their own family background.

1 · Segregation

Schools differ more in results than in whom they enrol

If students were randomly distributed across schools, almost none of the variation in family status or achievement would lie between schools. In Ukraine, – of the variation in students' socio-economic status (ESCS) is between schools. Achievement is even more concentrated: – of the variation in mathematics scores lies between schools.

Knowing which school a student attends tells us a great deal about their likely score. The question is how much of this reflects the students a school enrolls, and how much reflects the school itself.

7 · Ideas

What could follow from this: four ideas for discussion

Each idea below is linked to something the data above showed. None of them is tested here.

These are ideas for discussion, not proven measures. Our data show an association, not a cause.

  1. 1Mix students from different income backgrounds in schools

    For example, admission rules under which a school does not select children by their family's wealth.

    Why A school's composition explains more than the status of a single family (see Section 2).

  2. 2Provide extra support to schools that enrol less affluent children

    Funding, teachers and tutoring.

    Why These schools have lower scores, and we cannot tell whether the cause is classmates or resources, so help is needed in both areas (see Section 1 and Section 2).

  3. 3Help talented children from poorer families reach stronger schools

    Transport, boarding places and scholarships.

    Why The village–city gap is largely explained by school composition (see Section 3). In addition, only 49% of the highest-achieving disadvantaged students plan to go to university, compared with 81% of advantaged ones.

    The 49% and 81% figures come from a separate study, "Why Some…" [add full reference], not from the PISA analysis on this page.

  4. 4Collect better data: follow the same students from age 15 to the NMT

    Track individual students from the PISA age through to their national exam results.

    Why Right now we can see a link, but we cannot prove what exactly would change outcomes (see Limitations).

Methods

Data and methods

Data

OECD PISA 2025 public-use student and school files. Ukraine participated with a sample covering 17 of its 27 regions (country code QUA); schools in occupied and unsafe territories were not sampled. The student file was merged with the school file by school ID.

Measures

  • Achievement: PISA scores in mathematics, reading and science (10 plausible values each).
  • Family status: ESCS, the PISA index of economic, social and cultural status. It combines parents' education and occupation with home possessions. 0 is the OECD average.
  • School composition: the weighted mean ESCS of the sampled students in each school.
  • School location: settlement size reported by the principal (SC001), from village (< 3,000) to large city (> 1 million).

Models

Weighted linear regressions: M1 score ~ ESCS; M2 score ~ ESCS + school mean ESCS; M3 score ~ school fixed effects; M4 score ~ ESCS + school fixed effects. Location models regress score on settlement-size dummies, adding ESCS and school mean ESCS. Standardised coefficients multiply the unit coefficient by the weighted SD of student ESCS or of school mean ESCS across students.

Estimation

All estimates follow the PISA Data Analysis Manual. Each model is estimated with the final student weight for each of the 10 plausible values. Sampling variance is computed from the 80 Fay BRR replicate weights (k = 0.5), and the results are combined with Rubin's rules. School mean ESCS is recomputed under each replicate weight. Differences are treated as statistically significant when |estimate| > 1.96 × SE.

Limitations

  • The estimates are associations, not causal effects. School composition is partly the result of families choosing schools and schools selecting students, so it also captures unmeasured differences between families.
  • School mean ESCS is estimated from the sampled students (about 25 per school), including the student themselves. Measurement error in this mean tends to bias its coefficient towards zero.
  • The sample covers 17 of 27 regions and is not representative of all of Ukraine. The public file contains no breakdown by oblast.
  • PISA measures the literacy of 15-year-olds, not NMT results.
